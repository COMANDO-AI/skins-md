# STRATEGY.md — MVP Direction

## Rector principle

The goal is not to make a prettier chatbot. The goal is to own the personalization layer for AI assistants: visual identity, emotional register, ritual, persona, and workflow packaged in a portable plain-markdown file.

Means are subordinate to that objective. If normal product-app CSS cannot deliver the sensory transformation, the technical path must move toward gaming, creative-coding, and real-time visual systems while preserving usability.

## Research synthesis

### Technical path

The strongest MVP path is hybrid:

- Semantic React/DOM chat for text, accessibility, localStorage, markdown, keyboard use.
- CSS custom properties for the SKIN.md token system.
- Three.js/WebGL as a controlled sensory layer: particles, depth, scanlines, embers, aurora-like motion.
- Preset-driven visual configuration in SKIN.md, not arbitrary user code.
- Reduced-motion fallback and readable DOM text at all times.

This gives high sensory impact without making chat unreadable or turning the project into a game engine rewrite.

### Mass-user path

Mass users do not primarily choose AI by architecture. They choose by perceived usefulness, trust, vibe, identity, emotional comfort, and task context. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Character.AI, Pi, Replika, Poe, and similar products show that assistants are already perceived as different personalities even when interfaces are neutral.

The product wedge is:

> Give your AI a skin: a look, tone, and interaction world you can share, remix, and use anywhere.

### Why this can spread

Skins turn private AI use into visible artifacts:

- screenshots;
- share cards;
- creator collections;
- fandom-adjacent packs;
- study/work/comfort modes;
- “my AI setup” culture.

The creator pitch is simple:

> Make a skin for how your community uses AI.

## MVP implications

Build for immediate transformation, not configuration depth.

The first screen should make the before/after obvious. The skin picker should feel like choosing a vibe/class/environment, not editing settings.

Launch skins should balance utility and shareability:

- Minimal — baseline clarity.
- Terminal Oracle — developer/gamer/cyberpunk signal.
- Vaporwave Mirage — screenshot-first sensory wow.
- Gothic Librarian — mass dark-academia/research/writing appeal.

Future launch candidates:

- Cozy Study Companion.
- Pokémon Trainer.
- Anime Tutor.
- Fantasy Quest Mentor.
- Creator Muse.
- Soft Emotional Reset, with careful non-therapy framing.

## Network development

Early alliances should target:

- AI productivity creators;
- study influencers;
- Notion/template creators;
- digital artists and interface designers;
- streamers;
- fandom/community owners;
- writers/research communities;
- prompt/persona creators.

The first external ask should not be “use our app.” It should be:

> Design one skin for your community.

## Technical constraints to protect

- Browser-only MVP.
- No backend.
- No telemetry.
- No arbitrary JavaScript in skins.
- No direct IP-infringing fandom skins in bundled launch set.
- SKIN.md remains human-readable and backward compatible.

## What changed from the prior approach

Prior direction: product-app theming with HTML/CSS variables.

Current direction: accessible product shell plus game/creative-code atmosphere. The UI remains usable and static-hostable, but the sensory layer is now designed for wonder: particles, motion, depth, scanlines, bloom-like glow, and HUD framing.

## Winamp / Webamp strategic addition

The Winamp Skin Museum shows that the skin collection itself must become the front door. The next version should not bury skins inside app settings. It should open with a searchable, shareable, randomizable gallery where each skin is a cultural artifact: preview, author, readme/story, download, share, and live experience.

The key product sentence:

> Winamp made a utility player feel like yours. SKINS.MD should make the AI assistant feel like yours.
